# ADR-003: Trace-Element Pools, Metallothionein Buffering, and Zinc-Copper Antagonism

## Status
Accepted

## Context
Trace elements (Fe, Zn, Cu, Mg, Se) act as essential slow-moving cofactors for monoamine and antioxidant enzymes (TH, TPH, DBH, GS, GPX). In particular, zinc and copper interact antagonistically via metallothionein (MT) induction, and iron homeostasis is uniquely bounded by obligate sloughing without regulated renal excretion.

## Decision
1. **Zinc-Copper Antagonism**:
   - Metallothionein (MT) is induced by free cellular zinc ($\text{Zn}_{\text{free}}$) and systemic inflammatory cytokines ($\text{IL-6}$).
   - MT binds copper with substantially higher affinity than zinc ($K_{d,\text{Cu}} = 0.3 \ll K_{d,\text{Zn}} = 2.0$), in accordance with the Irving-Williams series and empirical metallothionein binding constants (Bremner & Beattie, 1995; Tapiero et al., 2003).
   - High zinc intake induces MT, driving down free active copper ($[\text{Cu}]_{\text{free}}$) and selectively suppressing copper-dependent dopamine $\beta$-hydroxylase (DBH) activity.
2. **Iron Kinetics**:
   - Renal clearance of iron is kept negligible ($k_{\text{renal}} = 0.01$). Iron elimination proceeds via obligate epithelial cell sloughing ($\text{slough} = 0.02$).
   - Hepcidin downregulates ferroportin/intestinal iron absorption under inflammatory or high-store conditions ($\text{block} = \max(0, 1 - 1.2h)$).
   - Absence of an active physiological excretory pathway renders iron overload a cumulative risk, preventing fast escape without phlebotomy/chelation analogues.
